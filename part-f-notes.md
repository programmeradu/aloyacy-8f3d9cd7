## F2 evidence
- Whimpact (Ramboll 2019, commissioned by MaaS Global, 2018 data, ~70,000 registered users): Whim users PT share 73% (normalised) vs 48% Helsinki similar demographics; 63% in another comparison; taxi 2.4% vs 1%; 42% citybike trips combined with PT; "38% of daily car trips replaceable" is an estimate. Critique: self-selection (Univ. Sydney ITLS 30 Apr 2019). https://kollektivtrafikk.no/app/uploads/2021/04/Ramboll_whimpact-2019.pdf ; https://www.sydney.edu.au/business/news-and-events/news/2019/04/30/whimpact--more-questions-than-answers.html
- UbiGo Gothenburg Nov 2013 to Apr 2014: 195 users, 83 households; travel diaries before n=40 / during n=36: private car use -50%, carsharing +200%; recruitment targeted; project compensated participants for not using private cars (insurance, parking) and ~20/21 private vehicles deliberately unused. Sochor et al 2018 Transportation 10.1007/s11116-018-9946-8: 95% of car shedders and 53% of economizers used car considerably more seldom. Service ended (business model).
- Vienna SMILE pilot 2014-15, ~1,000 users (80% male): 48% increased PT, 21% reduced private car, 10% more bikeshare (Urban Transport Group MaaS report; Cerema)
- Jelbi Berlin 6-year whitepaper (BVG, Jul 2025): ~350 stations, ~60,000 vehicles, 1.1M downloads, 40+ partners; pilot hub networks: sharing use Biesdorf 13%->23% (2021->2024), Adlershof 15->23%; car use Biesdorf 46->37%, Adlershof 36->28% (own surveys, not controlled); 63% within 5 min of hub see sharing as good alternative; 33% of Jelbi/heavy sharing users willing to give up car vs 14% non-users; typical station EUR25,000 vs EUR700,000 for 50 garage spaces. https://www.jelbi.de/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Whitepaper-Ergebnisse-sechs-Jahre-Jelbi.pdf
- RTI: NYC Bus Time (Brakewood, Macfarlane, Watkins 2015 TR-C 53): +118 rides/route/weekday, median +1.7%, greater on frequent routes. Chicago Bus Tracker (Tang & Thakuriah 2012 TR-C 22): +126 rides/route/day, 1.8 to 2.2%. Seattle OneBusAway (Watkins et al 2011 TR-A 45(8)): perceived wait = actual with RTI; 91% users report less waiting (Ferris CHI 2010). Tampa experiment: usual wait ~2 min less for RTI users, lower anxiety.
- Soft measures: Möser & Bamberg 2008 (141 studies, weak designs): Cohen's h 0.15, non-car share 39->46%. Semenescu et al 2020 TR-D 102397 (k=41 controlled): 7% reduction in car modal share, g=0.163. UK large-scale PTP 8 areas: car driver trips -11% avg (Chatterjee 2009 Transport Policy). Smarter Choices (Cairns et al 2008): potential ~11% national traffic, up to 21% peak urban, best combined with hard measures.
- Whim: MaaS Global bankruptcy March 2024; ~10,000 monthly active users in Helsinki; 2022 losses EUR9.3M vs turnover EUR3.8M (Traffic Technology Today 21 Mar 2024). ITF/OECD: direct substitution of MaaS for cars unlikely early on. Storme et al 2020 TR-A 131: Ghent pilot 100 car-owning employees with mobility budget, limited car substitution. Hensher et al 2024 (Transp Planning & Tech): very limited evidence MaaS achieved societal objectives.
## F3 evidence
- Tallinn FFPT 2013 (Cats, Susilo, Reimal 2017 Transportation 44): PT use +14% within a year; car usage -5% (WEF summary; systematic review 2026 notes car km +31%); walking trips fell ~40%; macro-level FFPT alone +1.2% demand controlling for supply (Cats, Reimal, Susilo 2014 TRR 2415); rest due to priority lanes & frequency. Pre-FFPT PT share 55%.
- Luxembourg FFPT Mar 2020: LISER policy brief 2026-17 (Van Acker, Sep 2026): pre-reform fares already low (EUR2 2h/EUR4 day), farebox 8% of costs; daily car use 34.5%->29.9% (surveys Feb 2020 vs 2023, not causal); daily bus 11.2->12.7; train 7.7->10.5; car-only users: 54.7% still car only, 1.2% PT only, 14.7% car+PT, 17.5% car+PT+active; strongest gains social; needs push measures (company car reform, parking management). STATEC: commuting car share 72.3% (2011) to 69.3% (2021), PT stable 17.6%. OECD 2025: little evidence it reduced cars. Synthetic DiD (Env Resource Econ 2026): road transport CO2 -5.9%.
- Dunkirk FFPT 2018 survey: 48% of new users moved from car; overall car-to-bus shift 24% (EU UMO case study) - came with network redesign.
- Shoup 1997 Transport Policy 4(4): 8 firms, 1,694 employees: solo driving -17% (share 76%->63%), carpool +64%, transit +50%, walk/bike +39%, commute VMT -12%. FHWA summary cites 13% drive-alone trips. Christiansen et al 2017: paying for workplace parking reduces odds of driving 11-30% (FHWA/ROSAP synthesis 2026).
- TRL593 (Balcombe et al 2004): bus fare elasticity ~-0.4 short run, -0.56 medium, -1.0 long run; service elasticity avg ~0.4 SR (UK), headway elasticity ~0.5 (TCRP 95 ch 9: most sensitive when prior service infrequent hourly/half-hourly).
- Road safety: EC final 2025 (27 Oct 2026): Cyprus 45 deaths per million, +10% vs 2024, -13% vs 2019; EU avg 43. 2026 to date 34 deaths, 14 motorcyclists; speeding, phone use, under-24 drivers (Cyprus Mail 27 Oct 2026). itscyprus.cy series: 2019 52, 2022 37, 2023 34, 2024 42, 2025 46.
